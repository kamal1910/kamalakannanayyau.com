I was born and raised in R. Kombai, a small rural village in Dindigul district, Tamil Nadu, India. Today I lead software engineering teams and design AI systems in the United States.

The road between those two points was not straight. That is exactly why I want to share it.

## A government school and a big dream

I studied at a government school from 1st to 12th grade. Growing up in a rural village, access to educational resources and quality teaching was limited. In 11th and 12th grade, some subjects did not even have dedicated teachers.

My 12th-grade results were not as good as I had hoped. On paper, that could have been the end of my plan to become a Computer Science Engineer.

I never let it be.

## When one door closes, find another

With my marks, direct admission into an engineering college was out of reach. So instead of giving up on the dream, I changed the route.

I joined a three-year **Diploma in Computer Technology**, worked hard, stayed focused, and finished with more than 85%. That result opened a new door: **lateral entry directly into the second year** of an engineering college. I completed my engineering degree in three years instead of the usual four.

It was one of the most important turning points of my life, and it taught me something I still rely on: when one path is closed, determination and hard work can help you find another.

## A year of waiting, then a humble start

A degree did not guarantee a job. Breaking into the IT industry was its own challenge, and it took almost a year before I received my first opportunity, at a small company.

It was a humble beginning, but I treated every opportunity as a chance to learn, improve and grow. Through continuous learning, dedication and hard work, my skills and experience slowly compounded, and my career moved forward one step at a time.

## 2013: the American dream

One of my biggest dreams was to work in the United States and build an international technology career. In 2013, I moved to the US and turned that dream into reality.

A new country brought new opportunities, challenges, responsibilities and experiences. I kept learning and adapting, both professionally and personally. Over the years I took on greater responsibilities and grew into a software engineering leadership role.

## Still learning

My journey is still continuing. Technology keeps evolving, and learning should never stop. Today I'm most passionate about:

- Artificial Intelligence and Agentic AI
- Enterprise AI platforms and intelligent automation
- Robotic Process Automation (RPA)
- Cloud architecture and distributed systems
- Emerging technologies, including Quantum Computing
- Building innovative products that solve real-world problems
- Mentoring, sharing knowledge, and helping others grow

## What the journey taught me

1. **Believe in your dreams.** Your background, school, financial situation, or academic results should never define your future.
2. **There is always another path.** When one opportunity is unavailable, look for another way forward.
3. **Hard work and consistency matter.** Success is not achieved overnight. Every small step contributes to a bigger achievement.
4. **Never stop learning.** Education does not end with a degree. Continuous learning creates new opportunities throughout life.
5. **Stay humble and help others.** Remember where you started, appreciate the people who supported you, and help others along their journey.

## The person behind the code

Beyond technology, my greatest happiness comes from spending time with my wife, son, and daughter. Their happiness, growth, and future are among my biggest motivations.

I'm always looking for chances to learn, challenge myself and explore new ideas. I enjoy sharing knowledge, supporting colleagues and mentoring others as they grow in their careers. And I believe there is always something new to learn, build, and achieve, regardless of where you start.

## If you're starting where I started

I didn't have a perfect academic beginning. I faced challenges getting into engineering college and finding my first job. But I never stopped believing in myself or my dreams.

Looking back, I realize my journey was never about where I started. It was about the decisions I made, the challenges I faced, the lessons I learned, and the determination to keep moving forward.

You don't need a perfect beginning to build an extraordinary future.

> **Dream big. Work hard. Keep learning. Never give up.**
>
> Your starting point does not define your destination.
